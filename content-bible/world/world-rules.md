# WORLD / RULES

> What is true in the Aperçu universe. Genre, tone, and the laws that keep a hundred
> episodes belonging to one world.

STATUS / CANON · UPDATED / 11.09.26

## The premise

A timeless quasi-medieval world used to tell **contemporary stories about power,
money and attention**. It is not historical fiction — it is an editorial cartoon in
medieval dress. A story about an algorithm hoarding attention is told as a king
hoarding grain. The reader always feels the double meaning.

## Tonal register

- Wry, observant, humane. Never cynical, never cute.
- The comedy is in *recognition*, not in gags.
- Emil witnesses; the world reveals itself; the audience draws the conclusion.

## The two registers

The world exists in exactly two visual registers, and the contrast between them carries
meaning. They are fully specified in their own files:

- **Poor world** → `world/poor-world.md`
- **Rich world** → `world/rich-world.md`

Wealth is never shown by giving a character nicer clothes. It changes the **drawing
itself** — the density, the colour count, the symmetry, the camera. A scene *is* poor or
rich in how it is made.

## Fixed laws

1. **One protagonist.** Emil recurs; everyone else serves the episode.
2. **No modern objects on screen.** Modernity appears only as metaphor (a ledger, a
   grain tithe, a decree), never as a phone or a screen.
3. **Numbers are characters.** A statistic (94% of the grain) is staged as an event,
   not a caption — but it must be true or clearly labelled illustrative, exactly as on
   the website.
4. **The world is slightly crooked.** Nothing in the poor world is perfectly straight.
5. **Restraint over spectacle.** Small motions, hard cuts, held drawings. See
   `world/motion-language.md`.

## Recurring institutions (staging vocabulary)

- **The Crown** — concentrated power / platform owner.
- **The Ledger** — the record that decides who has what / the algorithm.
- **The Tithe** — extraction dressed as custom / rent-taking.
- **The Commons** — the many who are measured / ordinary people.

Use these as the translation layer between a real subject and the medieval staging.

## Output

Stories are written and shown in **German**. Titles may be bilingual later; the
architecture already supports it. Keep the medieval diction plain, not archaic —
readable German, not fake Middle High German.
