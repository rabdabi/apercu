# WORLD / RICH REGISTER

> How a scene is drawn when it belongs to the Crown. Wealth is excess *in the drawing* —
> more colour, more ornament, more symmetry, slower camera.

STATUS / CANON · UPDATED / 11.09.26

## Feeling

Overwhelming, ornamental, airless. The eye cannot rest — there is always more.

## Rules

- **Colour:** the full rich-world palette at once (`world/palette.md`) — gold, burgundy,
  deep-green, bone, vermilion. Density is the point.
- **Ornament:** illuminated-manuscript borders, gold leaf, repeated motifs framing the
  scene. Draw the border as part of the shot.
- **Architecture:** huge. Ceilings out of frame, long tables, long halls. Emil is small
  inside it.
- **Symmetry:** rich scenes are composed symmetrically. Power is centred and balanced —
  the opposite of the crooked poor world.
- **Objects:** excess — food, plate, servants, candles, hanging textiles. Fill the frame.
- **Shading:** modelled gold and drapery allowed here (still no photo-real gradients —
  gouache modelling, hard steps).

## Motion in the rich register

- Slow, deliberate camera moves (a long push down a banquet table).
- Characters move little; servants move at the edges.
- Time feels heavier here than in the poor world.

## Emil in the rich world

Emil keeps his plain poor-world costume and his crooked line. He is a **flaw in the
symmetry** — the one imperfect, undyed thing in a gilded, balanced frame. Never redraw
him to match. That contrast is the entire visual thesis.

## Canon checks (for `/apercu-canon`)

- [ ] Rich palette present; ornamental border drawn.
- [ ] Composition is symmetrical / centred.
- [ ] Architecture dwarfs the figures.
- [ ] Any commoner (incl. Emil) stays in poor-world costume and line.
